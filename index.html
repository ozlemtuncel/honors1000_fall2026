PhD in Mathematical Biology

Charlie Cameron

@Cgyc20

PhD student in mathematical biology at the University of Bath. I build hybrid simulation methods that bridge deterministic and stochastic regimes for spatially extended biological systems — applying fine-grained stochastic modelling where particle counts are low, and efficient PDE solvers everywhere else. My work centres on the Spatial Regime Conversion Method, a spatially adaptive framework that makes this switch automatically, without sacrificing accuracy at the interface.

00 The SRCM — Visual Overview
1 / 5

01 / overview

Spatially Adaptive Hybrid Simulation

The SRCM uses discrete compartment based methods when the species concentration are low , evolving via a SSA. Areas of high concentration are modelled continuously using PDE. The conversion threshold is concentration dependent with discrete processes converting particles between the different regimes. The detailed discrete methods are applied in areas of low particle count, otherwise an efficient PDE is used.

02 / motivation

Two Regimes, One System

PDEs are fast to run but cannot capture molecular noise. The SSA is exact but prohibitively slow at scale. Real biological systems — gene expression, would healing, morphogen gradients — naturally span both regimes at once.

03 / interface

Regime Conversion at the Boundary

At the PDE-SSA conversion threshold, continuous concentrations are converted into discrete particles — and back again. Mass is conserved between regimes, allowing a natural way of areas of different concentrations to be modelled using the most appropriate scheme.

04 / validation

Capturing the Discrete Wavespeed

The hybrid correctly reproduces the wavespeed of the discrete FKPP equation. By applying the SSA at the front of the travelling wave — where particle counts are low — the method preserves the stochastic slowdown that a pure PDE misses entirely.

05 / efficiency

Hybrid Scales. SSA Doesn't.

As particle counts grow, the SSA becomes increasingly expensive — runtime scales with the total number of reaction events. The hybrid scales with the PDE in high-density regions, keeping cost near-constant as the system grows.

01 Publications
Open Access Mathematics · MDPI · Vol. 13(21) · 3406 · 2025

The Spatial Regime Conversion Method

Charlie Cameron — Mathematics, Special Issue: Stochastic Modelling in Biology

Introduces the Spatial Regime Conversion Method (SRCM), a hybrid modelling framework enabling seamless transitions between deterministic (PDE) and stochastic (SSA) simulation regimes in spatially extended systems. Addresses the core challenge of reaction-diffusion processes where distinct spatial regions require different levels of description — deterministic in high-copy-number regions and stochastic where molecular noise is significant. The integrated SRCM-SSA toolkit is efficient, accurate, and unified.
